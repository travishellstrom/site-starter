# Turning an interview into captioned social clips

One recorded conversation can become a full interview page and several short clips. This guide covers the plain process. It uses no particular paid tool: any video editor that can add captions will do.

## Before the interview

- Ask your guest for permission to record, and tell them where it will be published. A short email is enough, and it is worth keeping.
- Record video and audio on the best setup you have. Use headphones, a quiet room and a camera at eye level. Record each person separately if your software allows it.
- Write down five or six questions. The strongest clips usually come from a story, a surprising fact or a direct answer to a good question.

## After the interview

1. **Save the full recording.** Keep the original file untouched in a folder with the date and the guest's name.
2. **Get a transcript.** Most video and meeting tools can make one automatically. Read the whole thing and fix names, places and numbers. Automatic transcripts get these wrong.
3. **Publish the full interview first.** Put the video (or audio) and the corrected transcript on a page on your own site. Give it its own title, a one-paragraph summary and a link from your `llms.txt` and sitemap. Every clip will link back to this page.

## Choose the clips

Pick moments that make sense on their own, without the question that came before.

- Aim for 30 to 60 seconds. Shorter is fine if the point lands.
- Start with the most interesting sentence, not a greeting.
- One idea per clip. If the answer needs a lot of setup, leave it for the full interview.
- Avoid anything the guest would not want repeated. Show them the clips if you can, or at least tell them which moments you picked.

## Make the clip

1. Cut the section in your video editor.
2. Make a vertical version, 1080 by 1920 pixels (9:16). Keep both faces in the frame if there are two people.
3. Add captions. Most people watch with the sound off. Use the editor's automatic captions, then read every line and fix mistakes. Keep each caption to one or two short lines, in a large, plain font with good contrast.
4. Add the guest's name and the name of your show or site in a small line at the bottom or in the first seconds.
5. Export as MP4. Also export the caption file (`.srt`) if your editor can, because some platforms let you upload one.

## Post it

- Write a short description in your own words: who is talking and what the clip is about.
- Put the plain link to the full interview page in the description or the first comment.
- Tag the guest, and let them know the clip is live.
- Post the same clip on each platform you use, one at a time, a few days apart. You do not need a new clip for every platform.

## Keep a simple log

A table with these columns is enough: interview, guest, clip title, date posted, platform, link, views after one week. After ten clips you will see which kinds of moments people watch.

## Clips for AI and search

The full interview page does the work for search and AI assistants. Make sure it has the guest's full name, a plain summary, the date, and the transcript as real text on the page. Captions burned into a video cannot be read by search engines. The transcript can.
